# Coffee Processing Methods

Processing — how the coffee cherry is turned into a green bean — dramatically affects flavor. It is often more impactful than origin alone.

## Washed (Wet Process)

The coffee cherry skin and all fruit mucilage are removed before drying. Beans are fermented in water tanks to break down remaining mucilage, then dried.

**Flavor impact**: Clean, transparent, terroir-expressive. Acidity is vivid and well-defined. Body is lighter. Sweetness is moderate. Tasting notes are precise — you taste the bean and its varietal character, not the fruit pulp.

**Acidity adjustment**: Washed processing adds +0.5 to +1.5 acidity points vs. the same bean naturally processed.
**Body adjustment**: Washed processing reduces body by –0.5 to –1.5 vs. natural.

**Best for**: Filter coffee, light roast espresso where clarity is desired, blends that need a defined acidic component.

**Common origins using washed**: Ethiopia (Yirgacheffe, Sidama), Kenya, Colombia, Guatemala, Panama.

---

## Natural (Dry Process)

The whole cherry dries in the sun for 3–6 weeks before hulling. The bean absorbs fermented fruit sugars during drying.

**Flavor impact**: Fruit-forward, winey, heavy body, lower acidity, high sweetness. Notes: blueberry, strawberry, red wine, tropical fruit, raisin, molasses. The "fermentation" character can range from clean and fruity to funky and vinous depending on process control.

**Acidity adjustment**: Natural processing reduces acidity by –0.5 to –1.5 vs. washed.
**Body adjustment**: Natural adds +0.5 to +1.5 body vs. washed.
**Sweetness adjustment**: Natural adds +1.0 to +2.0 sweetness vs. washed.

**Best for**: Espresso blends targeting chocolate-fruit profiles, milk drinks (the sweetness and body hold through milk), dark fruit dessert-style blends.

**Common origins**: Brazil (most production is natural), Ethiopia Guji/Sidama, Yemen.

---

## Honey / Pulped Natural

A hybrid. Skin is removed (like washed) but some or all of the sticky mucilage (the "honey") is left on the bean during drying.

**Honey types** (from least to most fruit):
- **Yellow Honey**: Most mucilage removed. Close to washed. Clean, moderate sweetness.
- **Red Honey**: About 50% mucilage left. Stone fruit, caramel, medium body.
- **Black Honey**: Minimal mucilage removal. Close to natural. Blueberry, chocolate, full body.

**Flavor impact**: The middle path. More body and sweetness than washed; more clarity and acidity than natural.

**Best for**: Espresso blends that need sweetness without heavy fruit fermentation. Very popular in Costa Rica and El Salvador.

---

## Wet-Hulled (Giling Basah) — Indonesia

Unique to Indonesia. Beans are hulled at high moisture content (20–50%), then dried again. This creates the distinctive heavy body, low acidity, and earthy-spiced character of Sumatran coffee.

**Flavor impact**: Very low acidity (–2 to –3 vs. washed equivalent), very high body (+2 to +3), earthy, herbal, tobacco, dark chocolate.

**Best for**: Adding body and an earthy low note to espresso blends at 15–30%.

---

## Anaerobic / Experimental

Cherries or beans fermented in sealed oxygen-free vessels. Creates unusual organic acids and esters — tropical fruit, bubblegum, boozy, spiced, fermented notes. Intensity depends on fermentation duration and temperature.

**Flavor impact**: Very high sweetness (+2 to +3), unique aromatic complexity, often polarizing. Acidity can go either direction.

**Use carefully**: The intense notes can easily dominate a blend. Use at 10–20% maximum to add a distinctive "signature" note.

---

## Processing × Acidity Matrix

| Processing     | Acidity effect | Body effect | Sweetness effect |
|----------------|----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Washed         | +1.0           | –1.0        | 0               |
| Natural        | –1.0           | +1.0        | +1.5            |
| Honey (Red)    | –0.5           | +0.5        | +1.0            |
| Wet-Hulled     | –2.0           | +2.0        | –0.5            |
| Anaerobic      | 0 to –1.0      | +0.5        | +2.0            |

These are relative adjustments from a neutral baseline; actual values depend on the specific lot.
