# Coffee Origins: Latin America

Latin American coffees form the backbone of most espresso blends. They are prized for clean sweetness, balanced acidity, and reliability.

## Brazil

The world's largest producer. Brazils are the base of most commercial and specialty espresso blends because of their low acidity, heavy body, and pronounced chocolate and nut character.

**Santos / Cerrado / Sul de Minas**: Natural or pulped-natural processing is common. Cup profile: dark chocolate, hazelnut, caramel, brown sugar. Acidity 2.0–4.5 (low), body 7.0–9.0 (heavy), sweetness 6.5–8.5. The low acidity makes it safe for milk drinks.

**Mogiana**: Slightly higher altitude than Santos. Cleaner, with more caramel and dried fruit alongside the chocolate notes. Good for blends that need Brazilian base with a bit more complexity.

**Natural Brazil**: Adds blueberry and raisined fruit on top of the standard chocolate-nut base. Useful for adding a fruit layer without using an expensive Ethiopian natural.

**Blending role**: Brazil is typically 40–65% of an espresso blend. It provides crema, body, and the chocolate-nut base note. Never the top note, always the foundation.

---

## Colombia

The archetype of "balanced" Latin American coffee. Smooth, clean, medium-high acidity, red apple, citrus, caramel, light chocolate.

**Huila / Nariño / Cauca**: High-altitude regions (1600–2200 m). Nariño is exceptionally bright and can rival Kenyan acidity. Huila offers more stone fruit. Cauca splits the difference.

**Profile ranges**: Acidity 5.5–7.5, body 5.0–7.0, sweetness 6.0–8.0.

**Blending guidance**: Colombia is the "bridge" component — smooth enough to blend with Brazilian base, complex enough to lift the cup. At 20–35% it adds clarity and clean sweetness. It softens the edge of an African component.

---

## Guatemala

Volcanic soils and distinct micro-climates make Guatemala one of the most complex Latin American origins.

**Antigua**: Clean milk chocolate, brown spice, light stone fruit. Balanced acidity (5.0–6.5), medium-full body (6.0–7.5), good sweetness (6.5–8.0). Excellent espresso component.

**Huehuetenango**: Higher and more remote. Cup profile: peach, apple, brown sugar, floral. More acidity than Antigua (6.0–7.5). Works well in filter and as an espresso brightener at 15–25%.

---

## Peru

Often overlooked. Clean, mild, good sweetness, light body, low-medium acidity. Grown organically at high altitude. Useful as a filler component that does not distract. Good for milk drinks at any percentage.

---

## Honduras & Costa Rica

**Honduras**: Full body, brown sugar, nougat, stone fruit. Medium-high acidity. Undervalued for espresso blends.

**Costa Rica**: Honey-processed lots are very sweet, medium acidity, clean finish. Tarrazu is the premium region. Good for single-origin espresso or as a sweetness-boosting component (15–25%).

---

## Blending Summary for Latin American Origins

| Origin    | Acidity | Body | Sweetness | Best role in blend         |
|-----------|---------|------|-----------|----------------------------|
| Brazil    | Low     | High | High      | Base (40–65%)              |
| Colombia  | Medium  | Med  | High      | Bridge (20–35%)            |
| Guatemala | Med-low | Med-high | High | Espresso body layer (15–30%) |
| Costa Rica| Medium  | Med  | V. High   | Sweetness boost (10–25%)   |
| Peru      | Low     | Low  | High      | Clean filler (10–20%)      |
