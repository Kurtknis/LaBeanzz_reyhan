# Coffee Origins: Africa

## Ethiopia

Ethiopia is the birthplace of Arabica coffee. Coffees from Ethiopia are celebrated for their complexity, floral aromatics, and vivid fruit character.

**Yirgacheffe** (Gedeo Zone): The benchmark for washed Ethiopian coffee. Expect jasmine and bergamot on the nose, lemon curd and stone fruit on the palate, and a tea-like, clean body. Acidity is high and sparkling — often 7.5–9.0/10. Sweetness is moderate (5.0–7.0). Best used at 10–25% in a blend to lift brightness without overwhelming.

**Sidama / Sidamo**: Slightly fuller-bodied than Yirgacheffe. Berry notes (blueberry, blackcurrant) are common in natural process lots. Washed Sidama is more citrus-forward. Acidity 6.5–8.5, body 4.5–6.5, sweetness 5.5–7.5.

**Guji**: An emerging region from southern Ethiopia. Natural Guji can read like fermented tropical fruit — papaya, mango, wine-like. High sweetness (7.0–9.0), moderate acidity, medium body.

**Jimma / Limu**: Lower altitude, earthier cup. Less floral, more herbal and spiced. Useful in espresso blends to add complexity without dominating.

**Blending guidance**: Ethiopian beans carry a blend's aromatic layer. Because they are delicate, use washed Yirgacheffe for precision; use natural Sidama or Guji when you want fruit intensity. Keep percentage below 30% in milk-forward espresso blends — acidity and florals can clash with milk fat at higher concentrations.

## Kenya

Kenyan AA and AB grades are the benchmark for high-grown East African coffee. SL28 and SL34 varietals dominate.

**Flavor profile**: Blackcurrant, tomato, red wine, citrus peel, brown sugar. Acidity is sharp and wine-like (7.0–9.0), body is full (6.0–8.0), sweetness moderate to high (6.0–8.0). The acidity is "juicy" rather than sharp.

**Processing**: Mostly washed (double-fermented wet process). The long fermentation contributes to Kenya's distinctive wine and berry notes.

**Blending guidance**: Kenya adds body and savory complexity that Ethiopian coffee lacks. A Kenya + Ethiopia combination covers both floral/citrus and fruit/wine dimensions. At 15–30%, Kenya lifts an espresso blend without making it sour. Avoid heavy darkening past Medium-Dark — it kills the varietal character.

## Rwanda & Burundi

High altitude (1700–2000 m), volcanic soils. Cup profile sits between Ethiopia and Kenya: clean stone fruit (peach, apricot), floral top notes, medium-high acidity (6.0–7.5), medium body (5.0–6.5), good sweetness (6.0–7.5). Excellent as a single-origin filter or at 15–25% in a blend to add cleanness and sweetness.

---

## Tanzania

Peaberry Tanzania (single-seed cherry) is denser and more intensely flavoured than flat-bean lots. Expect black tea, citrus peel, and dried berry. Good entry point for blenders who want East African character with slightly lower acidity than Kenya.
