# Roast Levels and Their Effects on Coffee Flavor

Roasting transforms green coffee beans through the application of heat. The degree of roast affects flavor, acidity, body, and sweetness in predictable ways.

## Light Roast

**Internal bean temperature**: 196–205°C (first crack, not past it).
**Visual**: Light brown, no surface oil. Dense, dry bean.

**Flavor effects**:
- Preserves origin character and varietal notes (floral, fruit, tea-like).
- Highest acidity of any roast level — malic and citric acids are largely intact.
- Sweetness peaks at light roast when sugars are partially caramelized but not degraded.
- Body is lighter — the bean's cellular structure is less developed.
- Bright, complex, terroir-expressive.

**Numeric adjustments from green baseline**:
- Acidity: +1.5 to +2.5 (highest)
- Body: –1.0 to –1.5 (lightest)
- Sweetness: +0.5 to +1.5

**Best for**: Filter, pour-over, cold brew designed to highlight origin. Washed Ethiopians and Kenyans roasted light are reference cups.

---

## Medium-Light Roast

**Temperature**: 205–210°C.

**Flavor effects**:
- Balance starts to shift from origin-forward to roast-influenced.
- Stone fruit and caramel notes emerge alongside varietal character.
- Acidity softens slightly; body increases slightly.
- The "sweet spot" for many specialty espresso blends.

**Numeric adjustments**:
- Acidity: +0.5 to +1.5
- Body: –0.5 to 0
- Sweetness: +0.5 to +1.0

---

## Medium Roast

**Temperature**: 210–215°C.

**Flavor effects**:
- Caramel and brown sugar dominate. Chocolate notes emerge.
- Acidity is moderate — present but not sharp.
- Body is medium-full.
- Origin character is still present but softened.
- The most commercially popular roast level.

**Numeric adjustments** (from green baseline, used as reference):
- Acidity: 0 (baseline)
- Body: 0 (baseline)
- Sweetness: 0 (baseline)

---

## Medium-Dark Roast

**Temperature**: 215–222°C.

**Flavor effects**:
- Dark chocolate, bittersweet caramel, smoky top notes.
- Acidity is low — most volatile acids have degraded.
- Body is high, mouthfeel is heavier.
- Origin character largely replaced by roast character.
- Sweetness decreases as sugars begin to carbonize.

**Numeric adjustments**:
- Acidity: –1.5 to –2.0
- Body: +1.0 to +1.5
- Sweetness: –0.5 to –1.0

---

## Dark Roast

**Temperature**: 222–230°C (into or past second crack).
**Visual**: Oily surface (oils migrate out of bean).

**Flavor effects**:
- Bitter, roasty, smoky, ashy. Chocolate is now dark/bitter rather than milk chocolate.
- Very low acidity (the original acids have almost entirely degraded; carbonic and other roast acids replace them, contributing bitterness).
- Very high body, heavy mouthfeel.
- Sweetness low — sugars are carbonized.
- Origin character is almost entirely absent.

**Numeric adjustments**:
- Acidity: –2.5 to –3.5
- Body: +1.5 to +2.5
- Sweetness: –1.5 to –2.5

**Best for**: Italian-style espresso designed to be served with milk (the intensity cuts through), or for drinkers who prefer roast-forward character. Not suited for single-origin use.

## Roast × Flavor Interaction Table

| Roast Level   | Acidity | Body   | Sweetness | Dominant Flavor Notes           |
|---------------|---------|--------|-----------|---------------------------------|
| Light         | High    | Low    | High      | Floral, fruit, tea, citrus      |
| Medium-Light  | Med-High| Low-Med| High      | Stone fruit, caramel, floral    |
| Medium        | Medium  | Medium | Medium    | Caramel, chocolate, balanced    |
| Medium-Dark   | Low-Med | High   | Med-Low   | Dark chocolate, bittersweet     |
| Dark          | Low     | V.High | Low       | Smoky, bitter, roasty, ashy     |

## Matching Roast to Brewing Method

- **Filter / Pour-over**: Light to Medium-Light (preserves acidity and aromatics at lower extraction temperatures).
- **Espresso (specialty)**: Medium to Medium-Dark (higher extraction pressure can over-extract light roasts without dialing in precisely).
- **Espresso (commercial/milk drinks)**: Medium-Dark to Dark (intensity and body are needed to cut through milk).
- **Cold brew**: Medium-Dark (long extraction with cold water works well with lower acidity and higher body).
- **French press**: Medium to Medium-Dark (full immersion benefits from body and lower acidity).
