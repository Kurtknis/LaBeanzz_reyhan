# Espresso Blend Archetypes

These are reference blend designs. Use them as targets when generating new blends. Each archetype describes the flavor goal, component strategy, and typical percentage ranges.

---

## Archetype 1: Classic Italian Espresso

**Goal**: Bold, bitter-sweet, heavy body, low acidity. Designed for milk drinks (cappuccino, latte).

**Profile targets**: Acidity 2.5–4.0, Body 8.0–9.5, Sweetness 5.5–7.0.

**Component strategy**:
- 55–65% Brazil natural (Catuai or Santos) — dark chocolate, hazelnut, body
- 20–30% Sulawesi or Sumatra Mandheling — earthiness, weight, molasses
- 10–15% Colombia Medium-Dark — smoothness, brown sugar transition

**Roast**: Medium-Dark to Dark.

**Notes**: This is the safest blend for milk-dominant menus. The Italian profile does not need high acidity — it needs body and bitterness to cut through dairy. Avoid any Ethiopian or Kenyan component; their fruit notes become sour in milk.

---

## Archetype 2: Chocolate-Forward Specialty Espresso

**Goal**: Milk chocolate, caramel, brown sugar, light stone fruit. Balanced acidity. Good as straight shot and with milk.

**Profile targets**: Acidity 4.5–6.0, Body 7.0–8.5, Sweetness 7.0–8.5.

**Component strategy**:
- 50–60% Brazil natural/honey — chocolate, sweetness, body
- 20–30% Colombia or Guatemala — balance, clean sweetness, light fruit
- 15–20% Ethiopia natural or Kenya — fruit complexity, brightness

**Roast**: Medium.

**Notes**: The most commercially popular specialty espresso profile. The Ethiopian or Kenyan component should be kept at 15–20% so its brightness enhances rather than dominates. This blend works well for both espresso and milk drinks.

---

## Archetype 3: Fruit-Forward Specialty Espresso

**Goal**: Red berry, stone fruit, citrus, wine-like brightness. Designed for the specialty drinker who enjoys a bright, complex shot.

**Profile targets**: Acidity 6.0–7.5, Body 5.5–7.0, Sweetness 6.5–8.0.

**Component strategy**:
- 35–45% Brazil natural — body base, chocolate undertone
- 20–30% Ethiopia washed (Yirgacheffe or Sidama) — bright citrus, floral
- 20–25% Kenya or Rwanda — berry, stone fruit, wine
- Optional: 5–10% natural Ethiopia for added fruit intensity

**Roast**: Medium-Light to Medium.

**Notes**: Not recommended for milk drinks — the high acidity can become harsh in combination with milk fat. Best served as a straight espresso or over ice.

---

## Archetype 4: Balanced All-Rounder

**Goal**: Approachable, clean, balanced. No single note dominates. Works for espresso and filter.

**Profile targets**: Acidity 5.0–6.5, Body 6.0–7.5, Sweetness 6.5–8.0.

**Component strategy**:
- 45–55% Colombia — the bridge origin; balanced by definition
- 25–35% Brazil — body and sweetness anchor
- 15–25% Guatemala or Costa Rica honey — adds sweetness and mild complexity

**Roast**: Medium.

**Notes**: The easiest profile to achieve with a 2–3 component blend. Colombia as the dominant component does most of the balancing work. This is the right starting point for a first LaBeans blend.

---

## Archetype 5: Heavy-Body Cold Brew Base

**Goal**: Low acidity, very full body, chocolate-molasses, works well extracted cold over 12–18 hours.

**Profile targets**: Acidity 2.0–4.0, Body 8.5–9.5, Sweetness 5.5–7.0.

**Component strategy**:
- 40–50% Sumatra Mandheling (wet-hulled) — maximum body, earth
- 30–40% Brazil natural — body, sweetness, chocolate
- 10–20% Sulawesi Toraja — spice, dried fruit, body
- Optional: 5–10% Indian Monsooned Malabar — additional body

**Roast**: Medium-Dark.

**Notes**: Cold extraction forgives earthy and heavy notes that might be aggressive hot. The absence of heat means acidity stays low even with medium-roast components.

---

## Archetype 6: Filter / Pour-Over Bright

**Goal**: Light, transparent, floral, fruit-forward. High acidity, clean, tea-like.

**Profile targets**: Acidity 7.0–8.5, Body 3.5–5.5, Sweetness 7.0–8.5.

**Component strategy**:
- 50–65% Ethiopia washed (Yirgacheffe or Sidama) — floral, citrus, tea
- 25–35% Kenya — berry, stone fruit, complexity
- Optional: 10–15% Rwanda or Burundi — cleanness, peach

**Roast**: Light to Medium-Light.

**Notes**: Avoid any Indonesian or dark-roasted component in this archetype — they will muddy the clarity. This profile requires precise light roasting and careful brewing.
