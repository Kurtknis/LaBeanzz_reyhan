# Brew Methods and Extraction

Different brew methods extract coffee differently, which means a blend optimized for espresso will not perform the same way in a pour-over. This document explains what matters for each method.

## Espresso

**How it works**: 7–10 bar pressure forces water through finely-ground coffee in 25–35 seconds. High pressure extracts oils, acids, and solids at a concentration 5–10× higher than filter coffee.

**What the blend needs**:
- Body: High (≥7.0) — pressure extraction benefits from dense, oily beans.
- Acidity: Moderate or controlled (3.0–6.5) — high acidity becomes harsh under pressure; it intensifies.
- Sweetness: High (≥6.5) — sweetness balances bitterness in a concentrated shot.
- Roast: Medium to Medium-Dark — lighter roasts can taste sour or under-extracted at standard espresso parameters.

**Key rule**: Every point of acidity in a blend feels 1.5× as intense in espresso versus filter. Design accordingly.

---

## Filter Coffee (Pour-Over, V60, Chemex, Drip)

**How it works**: Hot water (90–96°C) passes through medium-ground coffee by gravity over 3–5 minutes. Lower temperature, lower pressure, more selective extraction.

**What the blend needs**:
- Body: Light to Medium (3.5–6.5) — heavy body makes filter coffee feel thick and oily.
- Acidity: Moderate to High (5.0–8.5) — acidity is pleasant and refreshing in filter.
- Sweetness: Medium to High (5.5–8.5) — natural sweetness shines through without concentration.
- Roast: Light to Medium — the nuanced notes of light roasts are best appreciated in filter.

---

## AeroPress

**How it works**: Air pressure pushes water through coffee. Highly variable — can produce espresso-like concentration or filter-like cups depending on parameters.

**What the blend needs**: Versatile. Medium roast, medium body, medium-high acidity works well across most AeroPress recipes.

---

## French Press / Plunger

**How it works**: Full immersion for 4 minutes, coarse grind, metal filter (passes oils and fine particles).

**What the blend needs**:
- Body: High — the metal filter allows oils and particles through, adding natural body. Use this.
- Acidity: Moderate — coarse grind and lower extraction temperature soften acidity pleasantly.
- Sweetness: Medium to High.
- Avoid very light roasts — the coarse grind under-extracts them and they taste grassy or astringent.

---

## Cold Brew

**How it works**: Coarse-ground coffee steeped in cold water for 12–24 hours. Very low extraction temperature.

**What the blend needs**:
- Very low acidity (most acids don't extract at cold temperatures).
- Very high body — the long extraction time builds concentration from body compounds.
- Medium-Dark to Dark roast — the lower temperature means you need more soluble compounds.
- Natural-process and wet-hulled components work very well.

---

## Moka Pot (Stovetop Espresso)

**How it works**: Steam pressure (1.5 bar) pushes water through medium-fine ground coffee.

**What the blend needs**: Similar to espresso but can tolerate slightly higher acidity. Medium-Dark roast. Avoid very light roasts — the lower pressure under-extracts them.

---

## Brew Method Selector Summary

| Method      | Acidity target | Body target | Sweetness target | Roast level    |
|-------------|---------------|-------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Espresso    | 3.0–6.5       | 7.0–9.5     | 6.5–9.0         | Med to Med-Dark|
| Filter      | 5.5–8.5       | 3.5–6.5     | 5.5–8.5         | Light to Med   |
| Cold brew   | 1.5–4.0       | 7.5–9.5     | 5.5–7.5         | Med-Dark       |
| French press| 4.0–6.5       | 6.5–9.0     | 5.5–7.5         | Medium         |
| AeroPress   | 4.5–7.0       | 5.0–7.5     | 6.0–8.5         | Medium         |
| Moka pot    | 3.5–5.5       | 7.0–8.5     | 6.0–8.0         | Med-Dark       |
