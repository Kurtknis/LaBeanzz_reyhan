# Coffee Origins: Asia & Pacific

## Indonesia

Indonesia produces some of the most distinctive coffees in the world, characterised by low acidity, heavy body, and earthy, herbal, spiced complexity. The unique "wet-hulling" (Giling Basah) process drives most of this character.

**Sumatra (Mandheling, Lintong, Gayo Aceh)**
- Mandheling (North Sumatra): Deep, musty earth, dark chocolate, tobacco, cedar, molasses. Very low acidity (1.5–3.5), very heavy body (8.0–9.5), low sweetness (3.5–5.5). The classic heavy-bodied espresso base component.
- Gayo Aceh (Aceh province): Cleaner than Mandheling. More brown spice, mild fruit, still earthy but less pungent. Acidity 2.5–4.5, body 7.5–9.0, sweetness 4.5–6.5. Better for mixed espresso blends where you want the body without full earthiness.
- Lintong: Middle ground. Chocolate and herbs, moderate earthiness, good for espresso blends.

**Java (Kayumas, Blawan)**
- Estate-grown at high altitude. Cleaner than Sumatra, more chocolate and spice, less earth. Wet-processed Java is lighter and more similar to Latin American profiles.

**Sulawesi (Toraja, Enrekang)**
- Dark chocolate, brown spice, dried fruit undertone, full body (7.0–8.5), low-medium acidity (3.0–5.0), sweetness 5.0–7.0. More refined than Sumatra. Excellent espresso component.

**Flores (Bajawa)**
- Grown by smallholders at high altitude. Brown spice, dried cherry, full body, low acidity. Less earthy than Sumatra. Good as a 15–25% espresso component.

**Blending guidance for Indonesian coffees**: Indonesian beans add body and an earthy-spiced low note. In espresso, 20–35% Sumatra or Sulawesi delivers weight and mouthfeel without needing higher extraction. Avoid high percentages in filter (over 30%) unless the goal is specifically an earthy cup — the pungent earthy notes amplify with filter brewing.

## Papua New Guinea

Similar altitude and processing to Ethiopian washed coffees but profile is distinctly different: sweet potato, brown sugar, tropical fruit (guava, papaya), mild earthiness. Acidity 4.5–6.0, body 5.5–7.5, sweetness 6.0–8.0. Useful as a bridge between Latin American and Indonesian components.

---

## India

**Monsooned Malabar**: Coffee exposed to monsoon winds during open-air storage. This produces extremely low acidity (1.0–2.5), very heavy body (8.0–9.5), and strong earthy-musty notes. Used in tiny percentages (5–15%) to add body and mouthfeel in dark espresso blends.

**Mysore / Coorg**: Clean, balanced, mild acidity, chocolate and spice. More approachable than Monsooned Malabar.

---

## Vietnam (Robusta)

Vietnam is the world's second-largest producer, almost entirely Robusta. Robusta has roughly 2× the caffeine of Arabica, very high body, rubber/grain/dark chocolate notes, low acidity. Used in Italian-style espresso blends (10–20%) to increase crema, body, and caffeine. Not used in specialty blends.
