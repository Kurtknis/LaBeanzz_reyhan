# Coffee Varietals and Genetics

The varietal (botanical cultivar) of a coffee plant shapes its intrinsic flavor potential. Terroir and processing then express that potential differently.

## Key Arabica Varietals

### Typica
The original Arabica varietal from which most others descend. Cup profile: clean, sweet, mild acidity, light floral top notes. Low yield, susceptible to disease. Grown in Jamaica (Blue Mountain), Hawaii, and parts of Latin America. Moderate acidity (4.5–6.0), medium body (5.0–6.5).

### Bourbon
Mutation of Typica discovered on Réunion Island. Higher yield and more complex cup than Typica. Sweet, fruity (stone fruit, red berry), round, medium-high acidity (5.5–7.5), medium body (5.0–7.0). Red Bourbon is most common; Yellow Bourbon (Brazil, Colombia) has more sweetness; Pink Bourbon (Colombia) has the most complex floral-fruit profile.

### Caturra
Natural mutation of Bourbon. Compact plant, easy to farm, but thinner cup. Good sweetness, mild acidity. The base varietal for many Central American origins.

### Catuai
Hybrid of Mundo Novo and Caturra. High yield, disease-resistant. Cup: clean, balanced, mild, chocolate-nut dominant. Very common in Brazil (the "workhorse" varietal). Acidity 3.5–5.5, body 6.0–7.5.

### SL28 & SL34 (Kenya)
Selected by Scott Labs in Kenya in the 1930s. Responsible for Kenya's signature blackcurrant and wine acidity. SL28 is higher-acidity; SL34 is more balanced. These varietals thrive at high altitude with volcanic soil. Acidity 7.0–9.0, body 6.0–8.0.

### Gesha / Geisha
Originally from Ethiopia's Gesha forest, popularized at La Esmeralda in Panama. Extremely complex: jasmine, bergamot, tropical fruit, peach, honey. Very high acidity (7.5–9.5), light body (3.5–5.5), very high sweetness (7.5–9.5). Commands premium pricing. Use sparingly in a blend (10–20%) as a "top note" component or as a single-origin.

### Heirloom Ethiopian
Ethiopia has thousands of undocumented wild varietals collectively labeled "heirloom" (also called "JARC" after the Jimma Agricultural Research Center selections). This genetic diversity is the reason Ethiopian coffees have such varied flavor profiles even within one region. Cup profiles are unpredictable but typically floral, fruit-forward, and tea-like.

### Pacamara
Hybrid of Pacas and Maragogipe (the giant-bean varietal). Large bean, complex cup: stone fruit, chocolate, floral, herbal. Very popular in El Salvador and Nicaragua. Acidity 6.0–7.5, body 5.5–7.0, sweetness 6.5–8.0.

### Robusta (Coffea canephora)
Not Arabica. Twice the caffeine, rubbery/grain/dark chocolate notes, very high body, almost no acidity. Used in espresso blends for crema and caffeine. In specialty coffee, fine Robusta (from Uganda or Vietnam) is used at 5–15% specifically for body enhancement.

## Varietal Impact on Blending

| Varietal       | Acidity | Body | Sweetness | Dominant Notes              |
|----------------|---------|------|-----------|-----------------------------|
| Catuai (Brazil)| Low     | High | High      | Chocolate, nut              |
| Bourbon        | Med-High| Med  | High      | Stone fruit, red berry      |
| SL28/34 (Kenya)| Very High| High| Med-High  | Blackcurrant, wine          |
| Heirloom Ethiopia| High  | Low-Med| Med    | Floral, citrus, tea         |
| Gesha          | Very High| Low | Very High | Jasmine, tropical, peach    |
| Typica         | Med     | Med  | Med       | Clean, mild, balanced       |
| Pacamara       | Med-High| Med  | High      | Stone fruit, chocolate      |

**Rule**: When selecting beans by varietal for an AI blend, prioritize Bourbon and Catuai for base notes, SL28/Heirloom for brightness, and Gesha only when complexity and cost allow.
